                Frequently asked questions

                What's the difference between "Send a link" and "Send now"?

                "Send a link" uploads your files to VCPeer first, encrypted at rest, and gives you a link (or sends it by email) that works even when the recipient is offline — a free WeTransfer alternative, capped at 2 GB per transfer. "Send now" is a direct, browser-to-browser transfer with no size limit at all and nothing stored on our server — but both devices need to be online and the tab open at the same time.

                How big can the files be?

                "Send a link": up to 2 GB per transfer (shown honestly above the upload box; this is a server limit, configurable per deployment). "Send now": no size limit — files go straight from one browser to the other over a peer-to-peer connection, not through an upload server, so the practical limit is your device's storage.

                Is "Send a link" end-to-end encrypted?

                No — be honest about this one. Files uploaded via "Send a link" are encrypted at rest on our server (so a leaked disk or bucket isn't readable), which is what lets us generate zip downloads and image/video/PDF previews. The server itself can decrypt them. "Send now" is the stronger privacy option: file data streams directly between the sender's and receiver's browsers over an encrypted WebRTC data channel and is never uploaded anywhere.

                How does an expiring link work?

                Every "Send a link" transfer expires after 1, 3, or 7 days (your choice, 7 by default) or after an optional download-count limit — whichever comes first. A background cleanup job deletes the encrypted files once a transfer expires.

                Does the receiver need an account?

                No. Opening the link, or opening the email it was sent to, is all that's required.

                What happens if I close the tab during "Send now"?

                Both the sender's and receiver's tabs need to stay open for a "Send now" transfer to complete — there's no server in the middle to resume from. If the sender closes their tab, the link expires. "Send a link" doesn't have this limitation: once uploaded, the recipient can download it any time until it expires.
